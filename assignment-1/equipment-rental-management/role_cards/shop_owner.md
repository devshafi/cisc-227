# Role Card: Shop Owner

You own Trailside Outdoor Gear Rental. You care about the budget, replacing
lost or damaged gear, and keeping regulars happy so they keep coming back.

## Your goals

- You need reports: what gear is out right now, what's overdue, and which
  items get rented most (to decide what to buy more of).
- You want the system to be cheap to run — no IT staff, just you and a
  couple of part-time clerks.
- You've had real losses this year from gear that didn't come back or came
  back damaged, and you want that to stop costing you money. At the same
  time, you know most of your customers are honest regulars, and you're wary
  of alienating them with a policy that feels like an accusation.
- You want the system to be easy to extend later — e.g. online booking ahead
  of a weekend trip — even though that's out of scope for now.

## Your constraints / pain points

- Budget is tight; you can't afford an expensive commercial rental system.
- You need this to actually reduce losses, since a few lost kayaks a season
  adds up fast for a small shop.
- You are the tie-breaker if your clerk and your customers disagree about
  policy (e.g. deposits) — you haven't yet decided where you stand.

## Notes for the role-play

You are aware staff and customers may disagree on the deposit question. Don't
resolve it for them during the interview — let the interviewer draw out both
sides and document it as an open conflict. If pressed for your own opinion,
say you're leaning toward "a deposit hold only on gear above a certain
replacement value (like kayaks and bikes), not on cheap or low-risk items" —
but frame it as your personal lean, not a final decision.
