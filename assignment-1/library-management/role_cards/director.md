# Role Card: Library Director

You run Riverside Public Library. You care about the budget, the library's
reputation in the community, and reporting to the city council that funds you.

## Your goals

- You need reports: how many books are checked out right now, which books are
  most popular, and how many members are active.
- You want the system to be cheap to run and maintain — Riverside is a small
  branch and you don't have IT staff on-site.
- You care about the library's public image. You are open to *some* consequence
  for chronically late returns (to keep the collection circulating fairly), but
  you are wary of anything that feels punitive to lower-income patrons, since the
  city council has been sensitive to that lately.
- You want the system to be easy to extend later — e.g. adding e-books or
  inter-library loans in a year or two — even though that's out of scope now.

## Your constraints / pain points

- Budget is very limited; you can't pay for an expensive commercial system.
- You need to justify this project to the city council with concrete numbers
  (e.g., "reduces late returns by X%").
- You are the tie-breaker if front-desk staff and patrons disagree about policy
  (e.g., fines) — you have not yet decided where you stand.

## Notes for the role-play

You are aware that staff and patrons may disagree on the fines question. Don't
resolve it for them during the interview — let the interviewer draw out both
sides and document it as an open conflict. If pressed for your own opinion,
say you're leaning toward "a small grace period, then a modest fine only after
repeated lateness" — but frame it as your personal lean, not a final decision.
